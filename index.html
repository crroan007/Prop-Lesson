Fixed vs Variable Pitch

The DA42 uses variable pitch propellers — and understanding WHY is the foundation of everything else.

🚲 The Bicycle Analogy

Fixed pitch = stuck in 3rd gear forever. Starting is brutal, cruising is okay, going fast is impossible.

Variable pitch = a bike with gears. Low gear to start, high gear to cruise. You always have the right gear for the job.

🪶 What About Feathering?

In an engine failure, we want minimum drag. Feathering aligns the blade parallel to airflow — like turning a knife edge-on into the wind.

✈️

Fine pitch = low gear = takeoff. Coarse pitch = high gear = cruise. Feathered = blades parallel to air = minimum drag.

🤖 SECTION 2

FADEC & Set Point Curve

The DA42 doesn't have blue RPM levers. Instead, FADEC handles everything automatically.

🚗 The Automatic Transmission

FADEC is like driving an automatic car. You push the gas pedal (power lever) and the car (ECU) figures out what gear you need. No manual shifting required!

📊 How It Works

You set: Power lever position (% load)
ECU determines: Target RPM from the set point curve
Governor executes: Adjusts blade pitch to hit that RPM

📈

At 60% load, the propeller set point curve shows 1980 RPM. The pilot sets power, and the system delivers the correct RPM automatically.

🔧 SECTION 3

MT Propeller Construction

Know what your propeller is made of — and how to preflight it properly.

🏗️ What It's Made Of

Blades: 3-bladed, wood composite + reinforced plastic coating
Leading edges: Stainless steel
De-ice: Rubber strip guides fluid to leading edges
Spinner: Aluminum — NEVER push the spinner!

⚠️

AFM Warning: Avoid high RPM on the ground — stones and debris can damage the leading edges!

👋 Preflight Tip

Run your hand along the leading edge during preflight. Yes, you might cut your hand — but it's better to find damage on the ground than in the air.

DA42 with MT propellers — 3-bladed composite construction

✈️ Conventional Twin

Both engines rotate clockwise (from pilot's view). Same engine, same mount = conventional twin.

This means the left engine is the critical engine (PAST: P-factor, Accelerated slipstream, Spiraling slipstream, Torque).

🛠️ SECTION 4

Propeller Control System

Under the cowling — how the system actually changes blade pitch.

🧠 Brain & Muscle

ECU = Brain: Knows the power setting, calculates target RPM.
Governor = Muscle: Pumps oil to change blade pitch to hit that RPM.

How Oil Pressure Controls Pitch

⬆️
More Oil
= Finer Pitch
= Takeoff
⬇️
Less Oil
= Coarser Pitch
= Cruise
🚫
No Oil
= Feathered
= Engine Failure

⚖️ Counterweights

Centrifugal force naturally pushes blades to low pitch (flat, lots of drag — bad!). Counterweights on each blade base push them toward coarse/feathered (the safe direction).

🔒

Low Pitch Stop Pins prevent feathering below 1,300 RPM. That's why props don't fully feather on shutdown.

⚙️ Gearbox Facts

• Derates engine speed to prop speed by 1.69:1 ratio
• Governor uses gearbox oil to change pitch
• Check gearbox oil through the sight glass during preflight
• EIS shows propeller RPM, NOT engine RPM

💨 SECTION 5

Accumulator & Feathering

How the propeller transitions between feathered and unfeathered — and why the accumulator matters.

🔋 Pressure Accumulator

Also called the unfeathering accumulator. It stores pressurized gearbox oil in a nitrogen-charged metal cylinder.

Feather / Unfeather Sequence

🟢
Unfeather: Engine Master ON → accumulator releases oil → prop moves out of feather
🔴
Feather: Power lever to IDLE → Engine Master OFF → oil drains back → blades feather → accumulator stores oil
⚠️

Critical sequence: Power lever to IDLE FIRST, then Engine Master OFF. This is how you feather the prop.

🚨 SECTION 6

Emergency Procedures

The procedures that could save your life. Know these cold.

🔄 Oscillating RPM

1️⃣ Change power setting
2️⃣ Check for ECU failure (A or B)
3️⃣ Switch to other ECU
4️⃣ If unresolved → land at nearest airfield

⚡ Propeller Overspeed

Max: 2,500 RPM for 20 seconds!
1️⃣ Reduce power
2️⃣ Check ECU failure
3️⃣ Switch ECU
4️⃣ Land at nearest suitable airfield

🔒 Fixed RPM (Won't Change)

Same checklist as above. If ECU switch doesn't resolve → terminate the flight.

💀 Engine Failure Below 3,000 ft (MEMORY ITEMS)

  • Power lever — MAX
  • Maintain directional control
  • Airspeed — VSC
  • Gear — UP
  • Flaps — UP
  • SECURE the engine
🦶

Identify: Dead foot = dead engine. Verify: Pull throttle back. Fix: Engine master OFF (feathers prop). Secure: Alternator OFF, fuel pump OFF, fuel selector OFF.

🔄 Engine Restart — With Starter

Conditions: Under 18,000 ft (immediate) or 10,000 ft (2 min), under 100 KIAS

Power IDLE → Fuel ON → Alt air as required → Alternator ON → Engine Master ON (unfeathers) → Starter ON → Check circuit breakers

💨 Engine Restart — Windmilling

Airspeed: 125 – 145 KIAS (must be in this window!)

Power IDLE → Fuel ON → Alt air → Alternator ON → Engine Master ON → Prop unfeathers & restarts by windmilling
